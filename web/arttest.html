The same Builder, belt and Shippy drawn three ways on one clock. A is your images as the deck draws them now. B is 100% code. C is the hybrid: your deck art plus code for everything that moves, all on one 320×200 pixel grid.

What code nails

  • Everything moves for real. The gear turns pixel by pixel, legs bend at the knee, smoke and sparks are particles, lights flicker one cell at a time.
  • One pixel size and one palette everywhere, so nothing looks pasted on.
  • States are free. Jammed is just red plus shake plus sparks, no new drawing needed.
  • Tiny: all the art above is about 25 KB of code.

What we'd be missing

  • The painterly detail: hand-placed rust, worn edges, the chunky depth of your machines. Code looks cleaner and flatter.
  • Every new machine is real work to write, where the images are one prompt.
  • Big painted stuff (the deck, the rock underside, the skyline) is where images win hardest.

The plan stays hybrid: code for anything that moves (gears, lights, belts, crates, Shippy, smoke, sky), your art for the big still pieces, all snapped onto one grid.
